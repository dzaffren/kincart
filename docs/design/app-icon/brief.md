# Design brief — Shop-together

**Summary**: an older relative asks for something in plain words, and a family helper approves it from their own phone, so nobody hands over a password.
**Audience**: older shoppers (70+) on their own phone, and the adult child who helps. They meet the mark as a Home Screen icon, beside a notification, and at the top of the two pages.
**Brand adjectives**: clear, steady, kind   **Avoid feeling**: flashy, cluttered, childish
**Promise / big idea**: help from afar, with nobody's password changing hands.
**Competitive landscape**: shopping apps use carts, bags and price tags; payment apps use blue, shields, coins and cards; family-care apps use hearts, hands and houses; approval reads as a plain tick. All of these are off limits unless given a fresh form. PayPal's blues and its P are off limits outright. Open territory: everyday objects from an older person's life, and type that means "together".
**Mark type hypothesis**: a compact symbol first, because it lives mostly as an app icon and a 24px badge, locked up with a lowercase wordmark. Three types explored: letterform, pictorial, wordmark.
**Must work on**: iPhone and Android Home Screens (64px), Android's circle crop, a 24px one-colour badge, a 16px browser tab, light and dark.
**Constraints**: white mark on the violet tile, #5B3AA0 (`icon-tile` / `icon-mark`, D15). Wordmark in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, the system's typeface (OFL licence, logo use allowed), outlined to paths.
**Success criteria**: recognisable at 16px; not mistakable for PayPal, a cart app or a chat app; reads as clear, steady and kind.
**Decision-maker**: you   **Deliverables**: the mark, the lockup, the iPhone and Android icons, the badge.
**Assumptions I made**: the name stays "Shop-together"; the wordmark is lowercase; no tagline.
